12-hour vs 24-hour care

The most common question families ask is whether they need someone for a shift or around the clock. The answer usually depends on what happens at night.

What each option means

A 12-hour shift means a professional is present for either the day or the night, and the family covers the other half. 24-hour live-in care means someone stays in the home and is available across the day and night, with rest breaks arranged in practice.

Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on how much of the day genuinely needs supervision.

When a 12-hour shift is usually enough

  • The patient is mobile and can use the bathroom with minimal help.
  • Nights are mostly undisturbed sleep.
  • Family is present for part of the day.
  • Clinical needs are limited to scheduled medicines and dressing.

A day shift often covers the busier part — meals, medicines, hygiene, exercise, appointments. A night shift suits patients who are settled in the day but need help at night.

When 24-hour cover is usually needed

  • The patient is bed-bound, or cannot be left alone safely.
  • Confusion, wandering or repeated attempts to get out of bed at night.
  • Frequent night-time needs — turning, toileting, breathing support, medication.
  • A high-dependency or post-operative case with monitoring.
  • Family is not able to cover any shift reliably.

Think about the night specifically

Ask yourself a blunt question: if everyone else is asleep, what could go wrong, and who would notice? If the honest answer is "the patient could fall and no one would know", then night cover is the deciding factor — not cost.

Practical arrangements either way

  • A place to sleep and eat for live-in professionals.
  • A written medication and care schedule.
  • Emergency numbers posted where they can be seen.
  • A clear rule on who to wake, and when.
  • Rest breaks built into the plan — a professional who is exhausted is not safe.

How to decide

Write down a typical 24 hours and mark the tasks and risks hour by hour. Wherever the gaps are, that is the shift you need. It is common to start with a shift and move to live-in cover if needs change — the plan does not have to be permanent.
